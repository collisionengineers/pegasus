# Refinish And Corrosion Protection

The refinish scope deals with preparation, blend/colour match, masking or trim removal,
corrosion protection and final quality checks. It is never reduced to paint application alone.

## Refinish scope items

For each painted panel, the scope question set:

- **Substrate preparation** — what the surface needs before paint (bare-metal treatment, filler
  finishing, guide coat, substrate-specific primers; adhesion promoters on plastics).
- **Colour match and blend** — solid, metallic, pearl, or tri-coat; blend into adjacent panels
  where colour transition, finish type, panel position, or the paint-system method makes an
  edge-to-edge match unachievable. Blend operations are defensible on method and colour science
  — state the basis, and route paint-method disputes to `manufacturer-methods-evidence`.
- **Masking and removal boundaries** — what is masked versus removed; removal gives a better
  edge but costs MET time; the choice should be visible in the estimate logic.
- **Corrosion protection reinstatement** — see below.
- **Final quality checks** — fit, finish, colour under daylight, texture match.

ABP support (`extras-package.md`): paint protection lines, machine polish per panel, panel
repair sundries per repaired panel, and the corrosion-protection labour/materials lines.

## Corrosion protection reinstatement

Where corrosion protection has been disturbed by repair, require evidence of reinstatement to:

- Enclosed cavities and box sections (cavity wax).
- Seams and flanges (seam sealer, matched in bead profile where visible).
- Bare metal exposed by repair or stripping (etch/epoxy protection).
- Backside of welds and heat-affected zones.
- Underbody coating and stone-chip zones.
- Welded/bonded panel joints (weld-through primer; bonded-panel materials line).

*"A repaired panel is not complete unless corrosion protection, seam sealing and cavity
protection disturbed by the repair have been reinstated."* An estimate that renews welded
panels with no corrosion-protection or seam-sealer lines is under-scoped — flag it. Where an
insurer challenges these lines, they are repair-completeness items, not extras padding (see
`addendum-and-dispute-response.md`).

## Refinish defect taxonomy

When reviewing refinish quality (post-repair inspection, quality disputes), name the defect,
its cause, and whether correction is localised or requires repaint/blend:

| Defect | Typical cause | Correction character |
|---|---|---|
| Fish-eyes | Silicone/contamination under the film | Refinish affected area after decontamination |
| Lifting / wrinkling | Solvent attack on unstable substrate | Strip and refinish — not polishable |
| Orange peel | Application/atomisation | Flat and polish if shallow; repaint if deep |
| Dirt inclusion | Booth/preparation contamination | De-nib and polish; repaint if widespread |
| Runs / sags | Application | Flat and polish; repaint if severe |
| Sanding marks / mapping | Preparation showing through | Repaint affected area |
| Swirl marks | Polishing technique | Machine polish |
| Poor edge / masking lines | Masking strategy | Localised refinish, better boundaries |
| Colour mismatch | Match/blend strategy | Blend into adjacent panels — rarely polishable |

## Final QC prompts

Before an assessment treats a repair as complete: panel fit and shut lines, colour and texture
match in daylight, trim/badge/sensor refit, corrosion-protection evidence, calibration
certificates where ADAS was disturbed, road-test and diagnostic clearance, and a clean interior
handover. The QC and road test line in `extras-package.md` is the matching ABP item.
