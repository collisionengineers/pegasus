# Standard Lines of Attack and Defence

> **Source-workspace boundary:** This file is package-local evidence or an experiment only; it is not a Pegasus caller, policy, current instruction, or acceptance authority. `Pegasus.Core`, current operator authority, and an authorised human own every accepted fact, cost, category, outcome, legal position, send, report issue, and approval.


Walk through each of these on every new case. **Do not include points that don't apply** — overstating the case weakens credibility. Better to land six strong points than muddy the case with twelve weak ones.

## Use/omit gates

- Use **superficial/cosmetic damage** only where the photos, estimate, or invoice actually support
  that description. If there is structural, suspension, SRS, chassis, welded/bonded, or safety-system
  work, use a narrower repair-scope criticism instead.
- Use the **mainstream market** point only where the vehicle is not a prestige, performance,
  specialist, low-volume, collector, or provenance-sensitive model. For those vehicles, read
  `prestige-vehicle-market-stigma.md` and avoid overclaiming.
- Use **paint-depth / no physical inspection** where the opponent relies on undetectability,
  prior-history speculation, or absence of readings. Do not imply paint readings never matter.
- Use **desktop-only evidence chain** where the repair report and diminution report are both
  document-based and no expert has inspected the vehicle.
- Use **formula/proportionality** points where the report is driven by EVA-style repair-cost/PAV
  bands rather than comparable market evidence.
- Omit procedural defects, unsigned statements of truth, arithmetic artefacts, and variant mistakes
  unless they are actually present in the report.

---

## 1. Nature of the damage and repair

Establish what the damage actually was and what work was done, in plain factual terms. Goal: characterise the repair as cosmetic / superficial wherever the evidence supports it.

Check the underlying report and photos:
- Were panels **replaced** or **repaired**? Repaired panels retain original factory metal — stronger position than replacement.
- Were the affected panels **bolt-on outer skin** (wings, bumpers, doors, tailgates, bonnets) or **structural / bonded / welded** (sills, A/B/C-pillars, longitudinal members, inner wing structures, floor pans)?
- Was there any **suspension, steering, chassis, monocoque, airbag or safety-system** involvement? If not, state so explicitly — list each one ruled out.
- Was the vehicle declared **roadworthy** throughout?

State clearly what the work consisted of, then state clearly what it did NOT involve. Both lists matter.

---

## 2. The vehicle is not a diminution-sensitive variant

Diminution claims are most defensible for high-value prestige, performance, or low-volume vehicles where buyers pay a premium for unblemished history (Porsche, Ferrari, Bentley, AMG, RS-line, M-Sport, etc.).

For each case:
- Is it the entry-level spec or a performance derivative?
- Mass-market mainstream (Kia, Hyundai, Ford, Vauxhall, mainstream Audi/BMW/Mercedes) or prestige/performance?
- How many comparables does the EVA report itself record? High number = well-supplied market = buyer has choice = stigma argument weaker.

Use buyer behaviour as the touchstone: mass-market buyers focus on price, mileage, spec, service history, and warranty — not on whether minor prior repairs were carried out.

---

## 3. No physical inspection of the vehicle

Applies to virtually every EVA diminution report. EVA reports are desk-based — a calculation from documents alone. They never see the car.

State this directly. List what they did NOT do:
- Paint depth readings across repaired and adjacent panels
- Panel gap and alignment measurements
- Colour-match assessment under daylight conditions
- Inspection for overspray, blending edges, polish marks

Key argument: where the vehicle has no HPI marker, the **only** way a buyer would ever know the repair occurred is by detecting it on physical inspection. Without detectability evidence, the "permanent and measurable loss" assertion is unsupported.

**Where both the repair report and the diminution report are from EVA (desk-based throughout)**, state this explicitly — the entire evidence chain has never touched the car.

If the opponent responds by demanding CE paint-depth readings or a physical inspection, switch to
`paint-depth-and-prior-history.md` and `expert-procedure-and-evidence.md`; do not concede that CE
must prove a lifetime negative before the documented accident claim can stand.

---

## 4. Internal sourcing of underlying evidence

Applies when **both** the underlying repair report and the diminution report come from the same firm (common with EVA). The diminution report is the firm's commentary on its own earlier desktop assessment — they are marking their own homework. No external corroboration of either the damage characterisation or the repair cost figure.

Don't overplay — supporting point, not headline.

---

## 5. Qualifications and standing of the author

Two designations seen repeatedly on EVA reports:

- **CAE** = "Certified Automotive Engineer" — a paid designation available to individuals working in the automotive sector. Not an independently assessed competence accreditation.
- **AMIMI** = Associate Member of the Institute of the Motor Industry — a membership grade obtained on application and subscription, not a technical qualification specific to vehicle damage assessment.

Contrast with **ATA VDA** (IMI Automotive Technician Accreditation in Vehicle Damage Assessment) — the recognised UK industry standard, requiring independent competence assessment and periodic re-assessment.

Frame as relevant to the weight properly attached to opinions on repair methodology and market value — not as a personal criticism. Use hedging: "to our understanding," "in our understanding."

---

## 6. Stigma scale is not a recognised methodology

EVA's graduated stigma scale (3.5% / 6.5% / 15% / 22% bands based on repair cost as % of PAV) is an in-house formula. Not endorsed by:
- The Association of British Insurers
- Thatcham
- Glass's
- CAP HPI

The scale applies a flat percentage based **only on cost as a proportion of PAV**, taking no account of the **nature** of the damage. The same percentage applies whether the cost was spent on cosmetic bumper refinish or structural chassis straightening following airbag deployment. Real-world buyers obviously distinguish between these. The methodology cannot.

Where the numbers support it, add the proportionality point from `abi-benchmark.md`: the output can
become commercially disproportionate to the repair spend or exceed normal write-off marker
deductions without any observed market evidence.

---

## 7. The market multiplier

EVA's multiplier (1.25 / 1.15 / 1.0 / 0.9 / 0.8 depending on comparable count) is similarly unevidenced:
- No empirical basis for the specific bandings (5 / 10 / 20 / 30)
- No empirical basis for the increments
- A snapshot count on a single day on AutoTrader cannot demonstrate buyer behaviour

It is a further unevidenced adjustment layered on top of an unevidenced base figure.

---

## 8. Absence of supporting market evidence

The EVA report itself typically records (in its market evidence section) that keyword searches for "insurance loss" / "accident repaired" comparables returned **zero relevant listings**. They cannot find a single comparable accident-repaired example on the open market to support their own conclusion. The figure is entirely formula-driven — not derived from any observed market behaviour. Use their own admission against them.

---

## 9. Internal inconsistency against the ABI 20% benchmark

**This is the strongest standalone point. Include in every rebuttal.** See `references/abi-benchmark.md` for the full argument and worked example.

Short version: the EVA scale, at its upper bands, produces deductions exceeding the ~20% ABI guidance for Cat S vehicles — vehicles with permanent HPI markers and recorded structural damage. That inverts the hierarchy. A methodology that produces wrong results at its upper end cannot be relied upon at its lower end either.

---

## 10. Mischaracterisation of the repair

EVA reports often include: *"the repair cost recorded reflects the full scope of work authorised and should not be characterised as minor in nature."*

This targets the headline cost figure, not the work itself. Modern OEM parts, two-coat metallic/pearl refinish, glass-out operations, ADAS calibration, and four-wheel alignment are inherently expensive. Four-figure totals are typical for cosmetic front-corner or side-impact damage on current-model vehicles. **Cost is not a proxy for severity.** Spending £5,000 on bumper, wing, and trim refinish does not transform that work into structural damage.

---

## 11. Statement of Truth defects

**Only raise if applicable.** Check whether the EVA report's Statement of Truth is signed.

If unsigned:
- CPR Part 35 and PD 35 require an expert's report to be verified by a statement of truth in the prescribed form.
- PD 22 requires that statement to be signed by the maker.
- An unsigned statement of truth is, on its face, defective and does not comply with the procedural requirements applicable to expert evidence.
- Acknowledge the defect **may be capable of being cured** — that's honest and prevents opposing counsel batting it away immediately. The point still does work even if cured.

If the Statement of Truth is properly signed, do not raise this point.

---

## 12. Floating-point / arithmetic artefacts

**Only raise if applicable.** Some EVA reports include obvious computational quirks — e.g. "Combined Applied Percentage: 5.8500000000000005%" — the unrounded floating-point output of multiplying 6.5% × 0.9.

This illustrates that the figure has not been the subject of considered professional review before being placed before the recipient. It is the unedited output of an in-house calculator.

Include only where the report you're rebutting actually contains it. Do not fabricate.

---

## 13. Pre-existing condition

Where the underlying report records pre-existing unrelated damage or rates the vehicle as anything less than excellent / pristine, note this. Any notional buyer reaction must be measured against the actual pre-accident condition — not an idealised undamaged equivalent.

If the vehicle was rated "good" or "excellent" with no pre-existing damage recorded, skip this point.

---

## 14. Prior-history speculation and paint-depth burden shift

Use this when the opponent asserts prior repairs without evidence, or asks for paint-depth readings so CE has to prove there were no previous repairs.

Do not use this line where there is actual evidence of prior accident damage or prior repairs that must be addressed directly. In those cases, analyse whether the evidence affects PAV, causation, or quantum.

The answer is not that paint gauges are useless. The answer is that paint-depth readings are not determinative of lifetime repair history and cannot prove the absence of all prior cosmetic work. Read `paint-depth-and-prior-history.md` for the substrate, calibration, panel-map, and method-disclosure checklist.

Keep the burden point clear:

- The claim is based on the documented accident, repair scope, repair cost, and market/disclosure effect.
- If the opponent asserts historic accident damage, the opponent should identify the evidence.
- CE should not concede that it must prove a lifetime negative before its assessed diminution can stand.
- Owner evidence, dealer/approved-used provenance, clean history checks, and purchase enquiries can be proportionate evidence of known history.
- If the opponent frames the request as Part 35 questions, further expert evidence, or testing disclosure, read `expert-procedure-and-evidence.md` before responding.
