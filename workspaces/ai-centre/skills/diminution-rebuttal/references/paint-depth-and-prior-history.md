# Paint Depth and Prior History

Use this reference for Elcometer requests, paint-depth readings, physical inspection demands, previous-repair allegations, first-owner/previous-owner objections, and arguments that CE must prove no historic damage.

Source grounding:

- Elcometer 311 FAQ: `https://www.elcometerusa.com/311-FAQ.html`
- Elcometer 456 product information: `https://www.elcometer.com/en/coatings-inspection/protective-coatings-inspection-products/dry-film-thickness/digital/elcometer-456-integral-coating-thickness-gauge.html`
- Elcometer probe selection guidance: `https://www.elcometer.com/en/what-is-the-correct-coating-thickness-gauge-probe-for-each-coating-substrate`

## First decision tree

Before accepting or rejecting testing, answer these questions:

- What is the test meant to prove?
- Is that proposition central to causation, PAV, or quantum?
- Can the proposed test actually prove that proposition?
- Who is asserting the fact?
- Is there evidence for the allegation, or only conjecture?
- Would testing be proportionate to the dispute and likely value?
- Would testing clarify the issue or open a speculative side dispute?

If the request asks CE to prove that no previous cosmetic repair has ever occurred during the vehicle's life, reject that framing. CE should answer the documented accident, repair, PAV, and market-loss issue; it should not accept a burden to prove a lifetime negative.

If the request is really a demand for a new physical inspection rather than a clarification of the
served report, say so. Treat it as a new evidence request with scope, proportionality, cost, method,
and disclosure questions; do not let it become an assumed defect in CE's original opinion.

## Technical position

- Do not say paint gauges are useless. Treat them as tools that can check consistency and identify areas of concern.
- Elcometer's automotive FAQ says there is no single "right" paint thickness. Manufacturers adjust application for temperature, humidity, paint colour, and vehicle type.
- Elcometer's FAQ says the 311 F version works only on steel, while the 311 FNF version works on steel and aluminium.
- Elcometer's FAQ says there is no reliable non-destructive way to measure paint thickness over plastic with accuracy.
- Elcometer's probe-selection guidance distinguishes ferrous and non-ferrous probes and notes that a known sample may be required for calibration in some cases.
- Elcometer 456 materials support asking for probe type, ferrous/non-ferrous/dual FNF mode, calibration method, batches, readings, and reporting data.
- On vehicles with mixed aluminium, steel, composite, and plastic panels, any "full Elcometer analysis" must identify substrate and method panel by panel.

## Core response logic

- Paint-depth readings may raise questions about paint consistency, but they do not prove the absence of previous repair history.
- A bare possibility of historic cosmetic work is not evidence that the adopted PAV is wrong.
- If prior accident damage is asserted, the opponent should identify the evidence, the affected panels, and how that damage changes PAV, causation, or diminution.
- The documented accident and repair remain the operative cause unless the opponent proves a competing cause or valuation error.
- Owner witness evidence and dealer/approved-used provenance can be proportionate evidence of known history and reasonable enquiries.
- Do not overclaim provenance. It is a strong commercial anchor, not absolute proof that no minor cosmetic work ever occurred.
- Market stigma is driven by disclosure, provenance, history checks, repair scope, and buyer
  perception as well as physical detectability. A vehicle can have a market-history issue even where
  a paint gauge cannot conclusively prove every previous event.

## Minimum method disclosure

If an opponent relies on paint-depth testing, request:

- Gauge model and probe type.
- Measurement mode: ferrous, non-ferrous, or dual FNF.
- Calibration method, calibration date, foils/samples used, and whether a known sample was required.
- Substrate identification for every measured panel.
- Panel map showing every reading location.
- Raw readings, units, and any averaging or statistical method.
- Photos linking readings to panels.
- Explanation of why each reading indicates factory paint, cosmetic rectification, accident repair, or filler.
- Explanation of how the readings affect PAV or the claimed diminution.

## Solicitor-facing advice line

Use this mode before a formal insurer response:

- Explain that a court may ask what evidence supports the vehicle's history, but that is different from CE having to prove the impossible proposition that no minor cosmetic repair ever occurred.
- Recommend proportionate evidence first: owner statement, purchase enquiries, service/dealer provenance, HPI/history checks, and repair documents.
- Accept inspection only where it can resolve a real issue, with clear scope, method, cost responsibility, and disclosure requirements.
- If the insurer insists on paint readings, put the burden on the party relying on that analysis to produce a complete method and interpretation.

## Insurer-facing response line

Use this mode when drafting externally:

- State that the claim arises from the documented accident and repair.
- State that repair scope and cost are evidenced.
- State that prior-history assertions are unsupported unless proper evidence is provided.
- State that CE does not accept a requirement to prove the vehicle has never had cosmetic work.
- State that paint-depth readings are not determinative without correct substrate identification, calibration, panel mapping, raw readings, photos, and a reasoned link to PAV or diminution.
- Invite the opponent to provide evidence of any alleged prior accident damage and explain how it changes the valuation or diminution figure.
- Maintain the assessed diminution unless the evidence actually undermines it.

## Physical inspection demand response line

Use this where the opponent says a desktop diminution opinion is defective unless CE physically
inspects the vehicle:

- Identify whether CE's report is an expert report, solicitor advice, or a rebuttal of the opponent's
  own desktop report.
- If the opponent also relied on a desktop report, point out the symmetry: they cannot treat desktop
  evidence as sufficient for their claim but inherently insufficient for CE's response.
- A physical inspection may assist where condition, repair quality, colour match, panel gaps,
  overspray, or prior repairs are actually in issue. It is not a substitute for proving that a
  documented accident caused, or did not cause, a market loss.
- If inspection is proposed, require an agreed scope: who inspects, qualifications, panels, gauges,
  substrate method, photos, raw data, costs, and whether the result will be disclosed in full.
