# Defending CE Diminution Reports

Use this reference when Collision Engineers has prepared the diminution report and the user needs to answer insurer objections, solicitor concerns, written questions, or requests for inspection/testing.

- Read `evidence-reconciliation.md` before drafting.

- Read `expert-procedure-and-evidence.md` for Part 35 reports, addenda, written questions, or testing disclosure.

## Classify the objection

Identify the real objection before answering:

- Causation: the opponent says the claimed loss was not caused by the index accident.
- PAV: the opponent says the pre-accident value is wrong.
- Repair cost: the opponent disputes the invoice, estimate, or included items.
- Repair scope/severity: the opponent says the work was too minor to support diminution.
- Vehicle history: the opponent alleges prior damage, prior repairs, previous owners, or lack of clean provenance.
- Inspection/testing: the opponent asks for paint-depth readings, photographs, or physical inspection.
- Methodology/percentage: the opponent says the percentage or calculation is unsupported.
- Legal recoverability: the opponent says diminution is not recoverable.
- Expert formalities: the opponent raises Part 35, statement of truth, qualification, or report-content points.

## Core defensive frame

- Start from the true basis of the claim: the documented accident, repair scope, repair cost, PAV, and market/disclosure effect.
- Keep the issue anchored to the index accident unless the opponent produces evidence of a competing cause or valuation error.
- Treat prior-history objections as attacks on PAV or causation only where the opponent has evidence. Without evidence, keep the issue framed as conjecture.
- Do not concede that CE must prove the absence of all prior cosmetic work.
- Use owner witness evidence proportionately. The owner can speak to their knowledge, purchase enquiries, provenance, clean checks, and absence of known prior accident repairs.
- Use main-dealer or approved-used provenance carefully. It supports reasonable purchase history and market confidence, but it is not absolute proof that no minor work ever occurred.
- Acknowledge genuine limits. If CE did not inspect the vehicle, did not take readings, or relied on documents, state that and explain why the available evidence remains sufficient.
- Stand by the report only where the reconciled evidence supports it. If a defect is real, identify whether a corrected addendum is needed.

## Dealer/provenance nuance

Use consumer/dealer guidance carefully:

- Current CMA guidance frames material information around whether a consumer needs it for an informed transactional decision.
- Dealer guidance treats previous accident and/or insurance write-off history as information that may need disclosure.
- The same dealer guidance recognises that minor rectified damage, such as a paint repair to remove a scratch, may not be material in the same way.
- Do not cite dealer guidance as binding litigation law.
- Do not use dealer guidance to prove the vehicle had no prior damage. Use it only to support why clean provenance, approved-used purchase, and owner enquiries are reasonable history evidence.

## Solicitor-facing advice mode

Use this when advising the instructing solicitor before a formal response:

- State CE's recommended route first.
- Explain why CE should not be drawn into the opponent's evidential route if it shifts the burden to proving no historic cosmetic work.
- Identify the proportionate evidence to obtain: owner statement, purchase invoice, main-dealer/approved-used paperwork, HPI/history check, service records, and any photos.
- Explain litigation risk plainly: a court may ask what evidence supports the vehicle's history, but that does not mean CE must prove the impossible proposition that no minor cosmetic work ever occurred.
- Offer tactical choices after the recommendation: stand by the report with owner/provenance evidence; accept scoped inspection if the issue is genuinely material; or require the opponent to carry out and disclose its own method if it insists on paint-depth analysis.
- Avoid casual reassurance in written advice. Be practical, firm, and specific.

## Insurer-facing response mode

Use this structure for external responses:

- The claim arises from the documented accident and repair.
- The repair scope and cost are evidenced by the engineering report and final invoice.
- The PAV and diminution percentage are identified and proportionate.
- The prior-history assertion is unsupported unless the insurer provides evidence.
- There is no requirement for CE to prove that the vehicle has never had minor cosmetic work.
- Paint-depth readings are not determinative without correct method, substrate identification, raw data, and a link to PAV or diminution.
- If prior accident damage is asserted, invite the insurer to provide proper evidence.
- Confirm whether CE stands by the assessed diminution or whether a limited addendum will follow.

Optional paragraphs:

- Provenance: the vehicle was purchased through a main-dealer or approved-used route. That does not prove no minor cosmetic work ever occurred, but it is relevant history evidence and there is no contrary evidence of prior accident damage.
- Owner statement: the owner has provided, or should provide, a statement of their knowledge, purchase enquiries, and absence of known prior accident repairs.
- Test method: if the insurer relies on paint-depth analysis, ask for instrument, calibration, substrate identification, panel map, raw readings, photos, and reasoning linking readings to prior accident damage and PAV.
- Proportionality: proposed testing is not reasonably required unless it can resolve a real issue in dispute.

## Opponent report or testing challenge

Where the insurer provides engineer comments, paint-depth readings, or a testing-based rejection:

- Check whether it is presented as expert evidence and, if so, whether it satisfies PD35 report-content requirements.
- Check whether material instructions and assumptions are stated.
- Check who took measurements, their qualifications, supervision, equipment, calibration, substrate, raw readings, and panel map.
- Check whether the report distinguishes steel, aluminium, composite, and plastic panels.
- Check whether the conclusion links findings to pre-accident value or actual diminution, rather than only raising possible historic repair.
- Check whether the report considers adverse facts such as clean HPI/history checks, dealer provenance, owner statement, and the documented index accident.

## Drafting controls

- Do not copy case-specific narrative from source case materials into new matters.
- Do not overstate legal case law unless the cited authority has been verified for the proposition used.
- Do not present Highland or Trading Standards guidance as binding law.
- Do not call paint-depth testing useless. Say it is non-determinative unless properly scoped, performed, disclosed, and connected to the issue.
- Do not use casual internal phrasing in formal outputs.
