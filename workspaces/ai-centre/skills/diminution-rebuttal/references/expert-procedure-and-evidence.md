# Expert Procedure and Evidence

Use this reference when drafting or checking a Part 35 report, addendum, response to written questions, opponent testing challenge, or procedure-based objection.

Source grounding:

- CPR Part 35: `https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35`
- Practice Direction 35: `https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35/pd_part35`
- Practice Direction 22: `https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part22/pd_part22`

## Core rules to apply

- CPR 35.1 restricts expert evidence to what is reasonably required to resolve the proceedings.
- CPR 35.3 says the expert's duty is to help the court and that this duty overrides obligations to the instructing or paying party.
- CPR 35.4 requires permission for expert evidence. The application should identify the field, issues, proposed expert where practicable, and costs. On small claims or fast track, the court will normally permit only one expert on a particular issue.
- CPR 35.5 says expert evidence is normally given in a written report.
- CPR 35.6 allows proportionate written questions about an expert report. They may normally be put once only, within 28 days of service, and for clarification only unless the court permits more or the other party agrees. Answers are treated as part of the report.
- CPR 35.9 and PD35 paragraph 4 support disclosure of sufficient details where a party has information not reasonably available to another party. The disclosed document should give enough detail about facts, tests, experiments, and assumptions to permit proper interpretation and assessment.
- CPR 35.10 and PD35 paragraph 3.2 set the report-content checklist.
- PD35 paragraphs 2.1 to 2.5 require independent, objective opinion; no advocacy; consideration of material facts including adverse facts; clear limits where the expert cannot give a definite view; and prompt notice if the opinion materially changes.
- PD22 requires expert reports to be verified by statement of truth and directs the expert-report form to PD35. Witness statements use the PD22 witness-statement form, must be in the witness's own language, and the statement of truth must be dated when signed.

## Report or addendum checklist

For a court-facing expert report or addendum, check that it:

- Is addressed to the court rather than the instructing party.
- Gives the expert's qualifications.
- Lists literature, reports, data, photographs, valuation material, invoices, correspondence, and other material relied on.
- States the substance of all material instructions and facts.
- Distinguishes facts within the expert's own knowledge from facts drawn from documents or instructions.
- Identifies who carried out any examination, measurement, test, or experiment, gives their qualifications, and states whether the work was under the expert's supervision.
- Summarises any real range of opinion and gives reasons for the expert's own opinion.
- Gives clear conclusions.
- States any qualifications or limits on the opinion.
- States that the expert understands and has complied with the duty to the court and is aware of CPR Part 35, PD35, and the expert guidance.
- Includes the PD35 expert statement of truth where it is an expert report or addendum.

## Written questions and insurer requests

Classify the request before answering:

- Proper clarification question: focused on an ambiguity or missing explanation in the report, proportionate, usually within 28 days.
- New expert evidence: asks CE to perform a new inspection, test, valuation, or analysis beyond clarifying the served report.
- Speculative investigation: asks CE to prove a negative or investigate an unsupported allegation.
- Testing disclosure request: seeks the method and data behind a measurement or experiment already relied on.

Do not describe every insurer query as a Part 35 question. If the request is really a demand for new testing or a speculative fishing exercise, say so plainly and assess whether it is reasonably required.

If the request challenges competence, require a specific issue. General assertions that a desktop
review, CAE/AMIMI designation, or absence of paint readings makes the opinion invalid should be
answered by reference to the actual opinion given, the material reviewed, and whether further
evidence is reasonably required to resolve a defined issue.

## Desktop-only report chain

Use this where the opponent's repair report and diminution report are both based on documents,
photos, or system outputs rather than a physical inspection:

- Identify every report in the chain and whether the author inspected the vehicle.
- If no one in the chain inspected it, avoid language suggesting CE alone has a physical-inspection
  deficit.
- Ask whether the chain contains any direct evidence of repair quality, colour match, panel gaps,
  overspray, prior repairs, or buyer reaction.
- If the claimed diminution is formula-driven, separate the procedural issue from the methodology
  issue: the problem is not only desktop evidence, but desktop evidence plus an unevidenced formula.
- Use this as a weight point, not a personal attack on the author.

## Challenging opponent testing evidence

Where an opponent relies on paint-depth readings, physical inspection, or other testing, ask whether the material includes:

- The issue the test was meant to resolve.
- The person who took the measurements and their qualifications.
- The instrument model, probe type, and measurement mode.
- Calibration method, date, foils, samples, and any known-sample requirement.
- Substrate identification for each panel.
- Panel map and raw readings with units.
- Photographs linking readings to exact panel locations.
- Any averaging or statistical method.
- Interpretation that links readings to factory paint, cosmetic rectification, accident repair, filler, PAV, causation, or diminution.
- Adverse facts considered, such as clean history checks, dealer provenance, owner evidence, and the documented index accident.

If those elements are missing, treat the testing as weak or incomplete rather than as a decisive answer to diminution.
