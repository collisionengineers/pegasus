# Evidence Reconciliation

Run this check before drafting any diminution rebuttal, defensive response, or addendum. The aim is to stop document-pack errors from becoming opinion errors.

## Document inventory

- List every supplied file and identify its evidential role: instruction email, engineering report, repair estimate, repair invoice, valuation/PAV report, diminution report, photograph, correspondence, witness evidence, or raw metadata.
- Identify duplicate files by hash, filename, title, report reference, invoice number, page count, and matching figures. Do not treat duplicates as separate corroboration.
- Prefer the clearest filename in notes and mention duplicate attachment names only where the email chronology matters.
- Treat email threads as chronology, instruction, and strategy evidence unless the technical fact is independently supported by a report, invoice, photograph, or witness statement.
- Treat binary mail sidecars or Outlook metadata as no substantive evidence unless decoded content adds a separate fact.

## Timeline

Build a short timeline before writing:

- Incident date.
- Earliest engineering or repair assessment date.
- Repair start, on-site, or inspection date if recorded.
- Final repair invoice date.
- PAV valuation date and report date.
- Diminution report date.
- Insurer or opponent objection dates.
- Any outcome or settlement confirmation.

Use this timeline to keep the live dispute anchored to the documented accident and repair, not speculative historic issues.

## Figure reconciliation

- Extract PAV, trade value if available, mileage, condition, repair estimate, reserve, final invoice, and claimed diminution.
- Reconcile estimate to invoice and explain any increase or decrease instead of silently switching figures.
- Calculate repair cost as a percentage of PAV.
- Calculate claimed diminution as a percentage of PAV.
- Keep repair cost and severity separate. Expensive prestige parts, PPF, ceramic coating, geometry, and system reset work can make the invoice substantial without every item proving structural damage.
- If a third-party report quotes a different repair total, identify the source of the mismatch.

## Identity and valuation checks

Confirm that the PAV source, engineering report, invoice, diminution report, and photographs use the same:

- Registration.
- VIN if available.
- Make and model.
- Body style and derivative.
- Mileage.
- Accident date.

Wrong body style or derivative is a credibility issue. Flag it before relying on the valuation or using comparables.

## Photo classification

Label each image by use:

- Scene/context: confirms identity, location, or chronology, but not repair scope.
- Damage evidence: shows affected areas and severity.
- Repair evidence: shows work in progress or replaced/repaired parts.
- Post-repair evidence: shows final presentation, colour match, panel gaps, or finish.

Do not overstate poor-lighting scene photos as technical damage evidence.

## Defensive-use checklist

When CE is defending its own diminution report, identify the evidence already available for:

- The accident event.
- The repair scope.
- The repair cost.
- PAV and mileage.
- Vehicle market sensitivity.
- Owner knowledge and purchase/provenance evidence.
- Any prior condition or historic damage evidence.

If the opponent alleges prior damage, ask whether the allegation actually attacks PAV, causation, or quantum. If it is only conjecture, do not let it displace the documented accident and repair.
