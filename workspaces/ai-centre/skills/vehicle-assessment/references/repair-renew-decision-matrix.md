# Repair / Renew Decision Matrix

> **Source-workspace boundary:** Retain as candidate annotation/evaluation aid; remove prescriptive repair-policy voice and require accepted external domain rule. This is evidence, an example, a package-local format, or an experiment only; `Pegasus.Core`, current operator authority, and an authorised human own every accepted fact, cost, category, outcome, legal position, send, report issue, and approval.


The issue is not simply whether a panel *could* be repaired. The judgement weighs all of the
factors below; state the ones that decided the call, and flag borderline cases for the engineer.

## The twelve factors

| Factor | What it asks |
|---|---|
| Safety role | Does the part carry restraint anchorage, crash load, steering, or suspension duty? Safety-role parts lean renewal without method support for repair |
| Structural role | Outer skin, reinforcement, or load-path member? Structural members follow manufacturer method, not preference |
| Panel material | Steel, HSS/UHSS, aluminium, plastic, composite — material may prohibit repair (see `material-and-joining-cautions.md`) |
| Permitted method | Does the manufacturer permit repair/sectioning here at all? Route to `manufacturer-methods-evidence` where it matters |
| Access | Can the damage be reached without cutting or major strip? Poor access can make repair uneconomic |
| Hidden damage risk | Would repair proceed over unverified underlying damage? |
| Labour economics | Repair hours × rate vs part price + fitting + paint. Repair cost exceeding renewal cost decides itself |
| Parts availability | Back-ordered or obsolete parts change the economics and the timeline |
| Repairer capability | Does the nominated repairer hold the equipment/approval the method needs (aluminium bay, riveting, calibration)? |
| Finish standard | Can repair restore the pre-accident finish — texture, grain, gloss — on this panel in this location? |
| Corrosion reinstatement | Can disturbed protection (galvanising, e-coat, cavity wax, seam sealer) actually be reinstated after repair? |
| Previous repair or corrosion | Prior filler, misaligned earlier repair, or corrosion changes both feasibility and the baseline condition |

Standard wording: *"The issue is not simply whether the panel could be repaired. The assessment
considers access, material, safety role, finish standard, corrosion risk, labour economics,
parts availability and current repair-method constraints."*

## Fast triggers

The quick renewal-leaning triggers (torn metal, sharp crease through a swage line, broken
mounts, kinked skins) are in `damage-cataloguing.md` — apply those first, then use this matrix
on whatever remains borderline.

## Plastics — the detail

For bumper covers and trim, identify before deciding:

- **Substrate** — the material code (PP, ABS, PC blends…) governs repairability and preparation.
- **Tabs and mountings** — broken or stressed tabs often decide renewal even where the face is
  repairable.
- **Reinforcement behind the cover** — the energy absorber and crash beam are separate items;
  a repaired cover over a crushed absorber is not a repair.
- **Sensor brackets and radar/parking-sensor areas** — repair thickness and paint build can
  affect sensor performance; manufacturer method governs repair near sensor mounts.
- **Durability and economics** — a technically possible plastic repair still has to be durable
  and cheaper than a replacement cover, painted.

## GRP / composites

Ask for evidence of hidden bond fracture and delamination beyond the visible break, and
consider the stress/load path — composite damage travels along the laminate, not just at the
impact point.

## Borderline wording

State the assumption and invite override: *"On the supplied photographs the [panel] is assessed
as repairable at [X] hours; if strip reveals [specific risk], renewal at [part basis] becomes
the economic route. This remains provisional on the supplied evidence."*
