# Addendum And Dispute Response

> **Source-workspace boundary:** Retain evidence-gated drafting structure, remove any implication that a skill can set legal or settlement policy. This is evidence, an example, a package-local format, or an experiment only; `Pegasus.Core`, current operator authority, and an authorised human own every accepted fact, cost, category, outcome, legal position, send, report issue, and approval.


Use this when the user asks for an external response about a repair estimate, total-loss
position, ABP charge, storage/yard charge, PPE/consumable item, repair-specification challenge,
or a formal addendum to an earlier CE report. Set the technical position first; draft the final
wording through `ce-house-style`.

## Evidence gate

Check the underlying CE report, estimate lines challenged, damage photos, PAV/repair-cost ratio,
repairability status, storage dates, vehicle mobility/security, and any contrary engineering
evidence supplied by the opponent.

Do not defend a line item merely because it appears in a standard package. Remove or qualify any
charge that is unsupported, duplicated, stale, or not applicable to the vehicle condition.

## Addendum document structure

CE addenda follow a consistent, anonymised structure. Use these sections in order, dropping any
that do not apply:

1. **Instruction and relationship to the earlier report** — who instructed the addendum, which
   report it supplements, and what question it answers. The addendum supplements the earlier
   report; it does not silently rewrite it.
2. **Issue summary** — the disputed point in one or two sentences.
3. **Evidence reviewed** — the documents, photographs, estimates, and correspondence considered,
   each dated.
4. **Methodology** — the basis of the response: current ABP schedule, manufacturer method
   evidence, market evidence, measurement, or inspection findings.
5. **Itemised response** — for a multi-item dispute, a table per challenged item: the item, the
   opponent's position, the CE response, and the evidence/method basis. Concede items that are
   genuinely wrong; a selective defence is more credible than a blanket one.
6. **Conclusion** — the resulting position and any change to the original figures, stated
   plainly.
7. **Statement-of-truth boundary** — where the addendum is for court use, the expert declaration
   is the engineer's to sign. Draft the technical content; leave declaration wording and signing
   to the engineer, and flag Part 35 formatting to `ce-house-style`.

For a **salvage-valuation addendum**, keep the valuation opinion separate from the underlying
total-loss assessment, and frame open-market salvage value against any insurer-contracted
salvage rate explicitly — they are different bases and should not be blended into one figure.

## Repairable vehicle challenged as total loss

- Separate repair economics from salvage category.
- State the repair/PAV percentage from the report and why the repair remains economically viable.
- If repair costs are agreed on a fixed or contract basis, state that only where the report/evidence
  confirms it.
- Do not present a client settlement threshold or policy cap as an engineering defect.

## Vehicle alleged to be already total loss

- Ask for documentary evidence of any prior total-loss marker or category.
- Do not accept a prior total-loss proposition from assertion alone.
- If a marker is proven, use `vehicle-valuation` for value effect and `salvage-categorisation` for
  category implications.

## Recognise the standard TPI challenge patterns

Insurer cost-challenge letters (Verisk-style desktop reviews are typical) recur on four angles.
Check each against the actual assessment before responding:

- **Duplicated operations** — the same panel appearing as both renewed and repaired. If genuinely
  duplicated, correct it rather than defend it; if the lines are for different operations (e.g.
  renew skin + repair aperture), explain the distinct scopes.
- **Parts-reuse pushback** — assertions that undamaged sensors, brackets, or trims can be reused.
  Answer from the damage evidence: single-use clips, deformed mounts, calibration consequences, or
  manufacturer method. Concede reuse where it is genuinely supported.
- **Labour-rate challenge** — answer with the current ABP retail/non-contract basis and the vehicle
  class (standard vs prestige/aluminium), per `labour-rates.md`.
- **"The vehicle is roadworthy / would pass an MOT"** — roadworthiness is not the repair standard.
  The claimant is entitled to restoration to pre-accident condition; an MOT pass does not answer
  panel distortion, finish, or corrosion-protection scope.

## Repair/renew and blend challenges

- Answer repair-versus-renewal challenges from the factors in `repair-renew-decision-matrix.md`,
  not from cost alone; state the concealed-damage caveat where strip evidence is outstanding.
- Defend blend operations from colour match, metallic/pearl finish, panel position, and paint
  method (see `refinish-and-corrosion-protection.md`); route method disputes to
  `manufacturer-methods-evidence`.
- Treat safety-role components (towbars, restraint anchorages, steering, suspension) with renewal
  caution — do not concede repair on a safety component without method support.

## ABP, retail repair, PPE, environmental, storage, or yard charge challenge

- Explain the operation or condition supporting each challenged item.
- Use current ABP retail/non-contract charge guidance only for current-rate support.
- Treat ABP as a pricing benchmark, not as an exhaustive list of every process consumable that may
  be required.
- Short explanation patterns for the recurring extras: **BS 10125 compliance** (accredited-repair
  process cost, alternative to the VM-approval uplift, not stacked with it); **EPA/environmental
  and sustainability charges** (waste-handling and compliance conditions attach — check the
  repairer holds them); **vehicle care kit / sundries** (consumables genuinely used on the job);
  **methods/repair-plan charges** (methods review and ADAS identification actually performed).
  For each, ask what case evidence supports the condition before defending the line.
- For PPE, masking products, mixing cups, abrasives, and refinishing consumables, check that the
  item is required for safe/compliant refinishing and is not duplicated in paint/materials.
- For yard charge, explain internal vehicle movement or handling separately from manufacturer repair
  time, and defend it only where the assessment context supports it.
- For storage, defend only where the vehicle was non-driveable, insecure, airbag-deployed, had
  glazing out, had exposed HV/fire/water risk, or was held pending a category/repair decision.
- Do not use date-sensitive energy or supplier-cost notes as a standing justification unless current
  ABP/supplier evidence supports the charge.

## External wording

Draft through `ce-house-style` after the technical position is set. Keep the response factual:
what was reviewed, which charge or operation is supported, why it is reasonable, and what specific
contrary evidence would be needed to revisit the position.
