## Source anchoring

The current source is the **ABI Code of Practice for the Categorisation of Motorised Vehicle
Salvage, 28 May 2025**, which supersedes the November 2019 (v11) code. Cite the 2025 code; if an
opponent relies on 2019 wording, flag the version drift. The 2025 edition added a dedicated
high-voltage battery matrix (see `hv-battery-cases.md`), an expanded glossary, and a revised
dispute-escalation path in which the appropriately qualified person assumes the final
categorisation decision, with duplicate MIAFTR entry handling. Do not reproduce the code's
matrices or diagrams — paraphrase and cite.

## First split: whole vehicle or parts only

- **Cat A candidate:** severe damage/contamination where the entire vehicle and all parts should be
  crushed and nothing is suitable for reuse.
- **Cat B candidate:** vehicle body shell should be crushed, but some parts may be suitable for
  controlled reuse.

Do not use Cat A or B merely because repair cost is high. Look for severe structural compromise,
burn-out, contamination, flood/sewage/salt-water exposure, identity/safety concerns, or parts-reuse
limits.

## Second split: repairable salvage

- **Cat S candidate:** repairable salvage with structural damage.
- **Cat N candidate:** repairable salvage with no structural damage, but economic repair is not
  viable or the vehicle is otherwise treated as salvage due to non-structural damage.

Use the structural checklist before choosing between S and N.

## Minimum reasoning

For any recommendation, state:

- damage evidence reviewed
- repairability/economics status
- structural/non-structural finding
- special factors: fire, water, HV battery, motorcycle, contamination, identity, parts reuse
- missing evidence
- whether the category is provisional or final

## Common errors

- Treating every uneconomic vehicle as Cat N.
- Treating high repair cost as Cat S without structural evidence.
- Treating cosmetic damage as Cat N without explaining why repair economics make it salvage.
- Ignoring HV battery/fire/water evidence that may move the vehicle away from ordinary S/N analysis.
