# Wheel, Tyre, And Alloy Refurbishment

Use for challenges to alloy repair, wheel refurbishment, tyre replacement, tyre-pair replacement, or
quattro/all-wheel-drive tyre rules.

## Maintained source families

- Audi alloy repair, tyre replacement, quattro, and Thatcham/Audi wheel material.
- BMW alloy wheel method material.
- JLR wheel refurbishment and battery-test material.
- Mercedes wheel refurbishment material.
- Nissan tyre/alloy material.
- VW wheel/tyre material.
- Volvo wheel/tyre material.
- Tesla tyre-pair material.

## Evidence to check

- wheel type, finish, diamond-cut/polished/powder-coated surface, cracks, buckles, gouges, and prior
  refurbishments
- tyre size, axle, tread depth, speed/load rating, run-flat status, sidewall damage, and manufacturer
  axle-pair requirements
- AWD/quattro drivetrain sensitivity to tyre circumference mismatch
- whether manufacturer guidance allows repair/refurbishment or requires replacement

## Response logic

- Do not defend refurbishment where the source says replacement is required for the damage/finish.
- Do not defend replacement where damage is purely cosmetic and the source allows refurbishment.
- Where rules are brand/model-specific, say the exact current manufacturer position must be checked.
- For query responses, paraphrase the decision point and explain why the cheaper option may not be
  safe, method-compliant, or warranty-compatible.

## Steering rack or tie-rod challenges

Do not treat steering rack replacement as automatically required where a tie rod or tie rod end is
damaged. Check impact path, steering/suspension damage, manufacturer method, alignment/geometry
findings, diagnostics, and any engineer instruction before defending rack replacement.

Where replacement is supported, frame it as a safety-critical steering component decision based on
inspection and method evidence, not as a discretionary upgrade over a cheaper part.

**BMW:** maintained BMW AIR (Accident Investigation Report) material sets replacement-versus-repair
criteria for steering gears after accident damage, alongside criteria for related components
(wishbones, track rods, front axle support). Where a BMW steering-gear line is challenged, the AIR
criteria are the anchor — verify the current BMW source for the exact model before relying on a
replace/repair position.
