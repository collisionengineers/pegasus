# Report Structure

## Full expert diminution report

Use this order unless the user supplies a house template:

1. Instructions and scope — state that the report addresses diminution as a matter of market
   valuation, not a legal submission; read alongside the original damage assessment report.
2. Documents and evidence considered.
3. Vehicle overview and pre-incident condition.
4. Accident damage and repair summary — acknowledge competent repair where true (no workmanship
   criticism), then distinguish: a good physical repair does not restore an identical market
   position, and a repairer cannot replicate factory processes such as robotic seam joining and
   sealing or factory paint application.
5. Physical repair condition and any inspection limits.
6. Market condition, provenance, and disclosure.
7. Valuation evidence and market comparables.
8. Analysis of whether a residual market loss is supported.
9. Quantum and calculation method, if evidence supports a figure (see Quantum framing below).
10. Exclusion of depreciation and other factors — expressly exclude normal age-related
    depreciation, post-loss mileage accumulation, and general market movement; the assessed
    diminution relates solely to accident history.
11. Timing and recoverability (valuation perspective) — market value is assessed at the point the
    vehicle acquires an accident history; a sale is not required, value exists independently of
    realisation. Whether and when the loss is recoverable is a matter for the court.
12. Limitations, assumptions, and conclusion.
13. Expert declaration and statement of truth where required.

## Quantum framing

Assess quantum in this order, and state the basis in the report:

- The structured factors: vehicle type, age, and mileage at loss; pre-accident market position and
  buyer expectations; repair cost relative to pre-accident value; extent, nature, and visibility of
  the repaired damage; disclosure requirements and provenance history; typical private, trade, and
  retail buyer behaviour for comparable vehicles.
- Anchor the figure against the recognised ~20% deduction for a recorded structural write-off:
  a properly repaired, unmarked vehicle sits **between** accident-free parity and that write-off
  discount. State explicitly why this vehicle does not attract write-off-level discount, and why
  it is nonetheless not treated as an accident-free equivalent.
- Present the figure as reasoned professional opinion from observed market behaviour — never as
  the output of a fixed formula or arbitrary percentage. Give the £ figure and its approximate %
  of pre-accident value for context.
- Frame the loss as part of the overall diminution caused by the collision, persisting after
  physical repair — not a separate or automatic head of loss, and not duplicating repair costs
  (repairs address physical damage; this reflects market perception).

## Solicitor advice note

Use this when evidence is incomplete or the user asks whether a claim is supportable:

- short answer
- evidence reviewed
- strengths
- weaknesses and missing evidence
- recommended next steps
- whether a full report is justified

## Market-stigma review

Use this for a focused opinion on disclosure and buyer perception:

- repair and history facts
- what would likely be disclosed or discovered
- market sensitivity of this vehicle type
- evidence for buyer discount
- limits and recommended additional evidence

## Style

- Write in a plain engineer's voice.
- Separate fact, assumption, and opinion.
- Avoid advocacy phrases. The report should read as independent expert analysis, not a pleading.
- Ground the expert's standing in dual experience where accurate: qualified motor engineer /
  vehicle examiner **and** experience buying and selling previously damaged and undamaged
  vehicles — that trade experience is the evidential basis for buyer-behaviour opinions.
- Render finished reports via the `collisionrenderer` connector (`templateId: expert-report`)
  with `collision-engineers-design` layout tokens.
