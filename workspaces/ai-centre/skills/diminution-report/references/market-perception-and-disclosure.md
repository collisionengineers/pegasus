## Core principle

Diminution is a market question, not only a physical repair question. Buyer reaction may be affected
by disclosed accident history, repair scope, provenance, warranty route, vehicle rarity, and the
confidence a buyer has in the repair.

## Factors increasing stigma risk

- Prestige, performance, specialist, classic, imported, low-volume, or provenance-sensitive vehicle.
- Structural, welded/bonded, SRS, suspension, steering, ADAS, HV battery, water, or fire involvement.
- Manufacturer warranty, approved-used eligibility, or specialist buyer expectations are material.
- The repair history would be disclosed by seller paperwork, correspondence, or buyer questions even
  if no HPI marker exists.
- Limited comparable market supply makes buyers more sensitive to provenance.

## Factors reducing stigma risk

- Mainstream vehicle in a well-supplied market.
- Bolt-on cosmetic repair with no structural/safety-system involvement.
- Good repair evidence, clean history check, and no obvious quality concerns.
- Buyer focus is likely price, mileage, service history, warranty, and general condition rather than
  minor prior cosmetic work.

## Evidence weighting

- Live market evidence is stronger than a formula.
- A guide value may support context but cannot prove post-repair stigma by itself.
- A sale at a reduced price can be relevant, but only after checking timing, seller motivation,
  condition, mileage, and comparable market values.
- If market evidence is thin, state the limitation rather than presenting precision.
