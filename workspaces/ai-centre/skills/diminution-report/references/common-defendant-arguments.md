# Common Defendant Arguments

> **Source-workspace boundary:** Retain challenge/evaluation examples, not a canonical rebuttal or business position. This is evidence, an example, a package-local format, or an experiment only; `Pegasus.Core`, current operator authority, and an authorised human own every accepted fact, cost, category, outcome, legal position, send, report issue, and approval.


Use this before finalising a claimant-side report or advice note.

## "There is no recognised head of loss for inherent diminution"

Do not advance diminution as a separate or automatic head of loss. Frame it as a matter of market
fact — part of the overall diminution in value arising from the collision, assessed by reference to
observed buyer and trade behaviour. The legal characterisation is for the court.

## "The vehicle was restored to pre-accident condition"

Answer by separating physical repair quality from market perception. A proper repair may restore
function and appearance, but it does not automatically answer whether disclosed accident history
affects buyer behaviour.

## "There is no loss until the vehicle is sold"

A sale can provide evidence, but diminution is normally assessed as a market-value issue. If the
vehicle has not sold, explain the market evidence used and the limitations of that evidence.

## "This is normal depreciation"

Separate ordinary age/mileage depreciation from accident-history stigma. Do not claim a loss unless
the evidence shows a discount beyond normal depreciation factors.

## "This is double recovery"

Clarify that repair cost addresses physical restoration. Diminution, if proved, addresses residual
market value after repair. Avoid claiming both for the same unresolved defect.

## "Paint-depth readings are required"

Accept that readings can be useful in some cases, but reject any absolute rule that a diminution
opinion is impossible without them. The claim arises from the recorded accident and documented
repairs; the repair documentation confirms the scope of refinishing, and the market effect derives
from accident-history **disclosure**, not from micrometer variance. If the opponent insists
readings are essential, they are welcome to arrange their own inspection. Read
`physical-vs-market-condition.md` before drafting.

## "The buyer would never know"

Check the actual disclosure route: repair paperwork, seller obligations, owner knowledge, prior
correspondence, specialist buyer questions, dealer provenance, and history checks. Do not assume
concealment or non-disclosure.

## If the defendant cites case law

Stay on valuation ground. The expert does not assert a legal entitlement to diminution; the
opinion is confined to market-value evidence, and legal authority is a matter for the court and
the instructing solicitor. Do not trade citations in the report.

## Cross-examination readiness

A finished report should survive these four probes — check each before signing off:

- The opinion is valuation evidence, not an asserted legal entitlement.
- The absence of a completed sale does not prevent assessing market value.
- The quantum comes from professional judgment informed by market behaviour — not an arbitrary
  percentage or formula.
- Some repaired vehicles suffer no diminution; the opinion is specific to this vehicle, damage
  profile, and market segment.
