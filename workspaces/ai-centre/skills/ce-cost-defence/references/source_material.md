# Source Material — What to Examine

> **Source-workspace boundary:** Retain generic source-extraction checklist, remove advocacy language and 'strongest source-supported evidence' instructions. This is evidence, an example, a package-local format, or an experiment only; `Pegasus.Core`, current operator authority, and an authorised human own every accepted fact, cost, category, outcome, legal position, send, report issue, and approval.


The case files supplied with each job are **examples only and change every time**. Do not reuse any figure, name, registration, rate or operation from a previous job or from the documents that happen to be in front of you as "the answer" — always read the current job's files and extract this job's facts. This guide tells you *which areas to look in*, not what you will find.

The instructing party usually supplies up to four documents. File names vary; match them by content.

---

## 1. Our original report (Collision Engineers' assessment)

Look here for the foundation of our position. Examine:

- The **vehicle identity** — make, model, derivative/trim, registration, VIN, mileage, fuel type.
- The **nature and location of the damage** and the recorded **impact magnitude / severity**.
- The **assessed values** — engineer's value, retail/trade, repair cost, and whether the vehicle was repairable or a total loss.
- **Engineer's comments / report history** — note any updates, re-assessments, and especially any **additional damage found on strip-down** (a common, legitimate reason later costs exceed an initial desktop figure).
- The **pre-incident condition** rating and any vehicle-history check result.
- The **basis of assessment** (physical inspection vs image-based / desktop) — relevant when the defendant has also not inspected.

Use these to anchor the report's intro and summary, and to show the costs flow from a documented assessment.

---

## 2. Our repair specification / itemised breakdown

This is the evidential backbone for defending the cost. Examine:

- The **labour rate** applied and the **time basis** (e.g. work units per hour).
- The **itemised operations**, grouped by type: **New / R&R**, **Repair**, **Paint**, **Blend**, **Specialist**, **Quality Control / check**. Capture the work units (hours) for each group and the **total hours**.
- **Paint and materials** figures and how paint time is derived.
- **Parts** listed (especially any added after additional damage was found) and their prices.
- **Specialist / sundry items** (decontamination, PPE, diagnostic checks, EV power-down/up, waste/EPA charges, etc.) — these are frequently overlooked by a challenger and are legitimate.
- The **totals**: net, VAT, gross.

When the defendant says "there is no breakdown", this document *is* the breakdown — reproduce the relevant grouped totals in the report's hours/cost table.

---

## 3. The defendant's letter / engineer's comments

This defines exactly what you must rebut. Examine and list **every distinct challenge**, which commonly fall into these areas:

- **Labour hours** said to be unsupported, excessive, or not broken down.
- **Labour rate** said to be too high (compare with the rate in their own spec).
- **Paint / blend** operations disputed (e.g. "solid colour, no blend needed", "no images of blended panels").
- **Repair vs replace** decisions questioned.
- **Specific operations** said to be unnecessary or duplicated.
- **Evidence / images** said to be insufficient to support the work.
- Any **regulatory or methodology** point raised.

Capture the defendant's wording precisely so you can quote it in the rebuttal. Also note **who** raised it (in-house engineer, third-party engineering firm) and **whether they inspected the vehicle**.

---

## 4. The defendant's repair specification (if provided)

Often an Audatex / insurer print-out. Examine for the **points of difference** with our spec:

- Their **labour rate** and **total hours / total labour** vs ours.
- Operations they **omitted, reduced, or reclassified** (e.g. removed blend, cut repair times, dropped specialist items).
- Their stated **pre-accident condition, vehicle status and damage severity** (sometimes inconsistent with the damage).
- Whether their figures are **labour-only** or a full repair cost — a frequent apples-to-oranges trap; make the comparison like-for-like.
- Any **caveats** on their document ("TBA", "not authorised", "manufacturer times") that undercut its weight.

Build a clear, fair comparison: where they cut hours or operations, explain why those operations are necessary to return the vehicle to pre-accident condition.

---

## Cross-cutting things worth highlighting in the report

These recur across cost disputes and are usually strong, evidence-based points:

- The defendant **did not inspect** the vehicle, while our costs flow from an assessment and an agreed repairer's specification.
- The defendant offered **no alternative methodology and no contradicting manufacturer/Audatex times** — only assertion.
- **Additional damage found on strip-down** legitimately increases the figure beyond any early desktop estimate.
- **Blending is necessary** even on "solid" colours to avoid a visible boundary and achieve a pre-accident finish.
- **Specialist and safety operations** (EV power-down, diagnostics, decontamination) are required, not padding.
- Our breakdown is **fully itemised and verifiable**; theirs (if thin) is not.

Always ground each point in this job's documents. If a point can't be supported from the current files, either leave it out or ask the user.
